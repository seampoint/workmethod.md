# Owner-controlled method changes

The owner decides changes or explicitly delegates that authority. Never edit a method autonomously. For a requested change:

1. Read the applicable method and identify its owner and any explicit delegation covering method changes.
2. Draft and show the exact change. Obtain confirmation from the owner or authorized delegate, identifying the responsible owner. A general request to review or improve a method is not confirmation of an unseen edit. With the change, say once, in one plain line and with no extra question, what it lets happen. If more than one of these applies, say the one with the most at stake:
   - When it lets the assistant act with no one checking first, what could reach people or change before anyone sees it, and how to stop it: "A wrong figure would reach Harbor before anyone sees it; removing this line stops it."
   - When it takes a decision from a person a method names: "This takes the decision from Dana, and she will not be told unless you tell her."
   - When it has the assistant deliver an apology, bad news or anything about someone's job: "People can usually tell when no one wrote it, and a form apology can cost more than the slip."
   - When it sets a limit that protects money or safety: "I will follow this, but nothing stops a mistake of mine; set the limit in the system that moves it too."
3. Apply only the confirmed change. Preserve the previous version in file history, raise the method version if a rule or decision-maker changes, and independently read back the result. Keep every existing rule's number. A removed rule stays as a line reading `Removed in v<number>.`, and its number is never given to another rule. Marking a rule or a decision line `Fixed:`, or removing that mark, is a rule change; only the owner of the method that holds the rule makes it.
4. For a change to an existing method, or a new method holding a rule that lets an act go ahead without asking, record the person's decision and the authorized act in the applicable record, naming the methods and versions in force. A new method with no such rule needs no entry. A method with no record beside it names, in its The record section, a piece of work's record that holds these entries and acts under its rules, and readers look there. Record this for a rule that lets an act go ahead without asking, even with the owner present, because a later run relies on the entry. If a step cannot be completed, disclose it and follow the unavailability procedure.

Renaming, moving or deleting a method requires specific authorization for that act. A correction entry does not by itself authorize a method edit.
